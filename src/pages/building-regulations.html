Building regulations

The Building Regulations, explained

The legal minimum standards for the design and construction of buildings – and how to make sure your project meets them.

What are the Building Regulations?

The Building Regulations are statutory standards made under the Building Act 1984. They apply to most new buildings and many alterations, and exist to secure the health, safety, welfare and convenience of people in and around buildings, as well as energy efficiency and accessibility.

They are separate from planning permission. Planning is about what you can build and where; the Building Regulations are about how it must be built. You may need one, the other, or both.

What do they cover?

The regulations are organised into Parts, each supported by Approved Documents giving practical guidance on one way of complying:

BFire safety

Part numbering and requirements differ in Wales, which has its own Approved Documents. Approved Documents are updated from time to time – always check the current edition.

The routes to compliance

1. Initial Notice (private approver)

You appoint an RBCA, who submits an Initial Notice to the local authority. Once it is accepted the approver takes over responsibility for checking the work. The notice must be given at least 5 working days before work starts. When the work is complete and satisfactory, a Final Certificate is issued.

2. Full Plans (local authority)

Detailed plans are submitted to the council for approval before work starts. The council inspects the work and issues a Completion Certificate.

3. Building Notice (local authority)

A simpler route for small domestic work: you give notice that you intend to start, without submitting full plans for approval. Not available for most commercial work.

Rule of thumb: decide on your building control route before any work starts. Starting without one can mean opening up finished work for inspection or applying for regularisation, which is more expensive and not always possible.

What changed with the Building Safety Act?

  • Dutyholders. Clients, designers and contractors have defined legal duties to ensure compliance. Projects with more than one contractor must appoint a Principal Designer and Principal Contractor for the Building Regulations.
  • Registered approvers and inspectors. Private-sector building control is now regulated by the Building Safety Regulator. See What is an RBCA?
  • Higher-risk buildings. A stricter regime, with the Building Safety Regulator acting as the building control authority, applies to tall residential buildings and certain care homes and hospitals.
  • Competence and accountability. Greater emphasis on competence, record-keeping and the “golden thread” of information.

Gas and electrical work

Gas work must be done by a Gas Safe registered engineer. Notifiable electrical work must be done by someone registered with a competent person scheme (or be separately notified to building control). The installer should provide a compliance certificate within 30 days of completion, which we will ask to see.

Building over or near sewers

Building over or within 3m of a public sewer needs the agreement of the sewerage undertaker. Please tell us at the start if this applies.

This page is a general guide, not legal advice. The Approved Documents on gov.uk (England) and gov.wales (Wales) are the definitive source.
