How it works

From quote to Final Certificate

A simple, transparent process with one point of contact throughout.

  1. Request a quote

    Send us a few details through our quote request form – or call. Drawings help but are not essential at this stage.

  2. Receive your fee proposal

    We confirm a fixed fee, what it includes, how it is invoiced and our terms.

  3. Appoint us

    Complete our online appointment form with the site, client and duty-holder details. We use it to prepare your Initial Notice.

  4. Initial Notice lodged

    We submit the notice to the local authority. It must be in at least 5 working days before work starts – so please allow time.

  5. Plan check

    The design will be assessed against compliance with the building regulations, so any issues are picked up and fixed on paper, not on site.

  6. Site inspections

    We visit at the key stages (typically around three visits for a project like an office alteration, more for larger projects). Tell us at least 24 hours before each stage.

  7. Final Certificate

    When the work is complete and we are satisfied, we issue your Final Certificate – the document your solicitor, lender or insurer will ask for.

What we’ll need from you

  • Site address and a description of the works
  • Site and block plans (1:1250 or larger, showing public services within 6m of the boundaries)
  • Floor plans, sections and elevations; structural and fire information where relevant
  • Client, agent, Principal Designer and Principal Contractor details
  • Your intended start date
  • Notice of each inspection stage as the work progresses

Don’t start on site early. Work should not start until the Initial Notice has been accepted by the local authority.

Frequently asked questions

How much does building control cost?

It depends on the size, type and complexity of the project. We give you a fixed fee proposal before you commit, normally invoiced in two instalments: on submission of the Initial Notice and after works commence.

How quickly can you start?

Quotes are normally turned round quickly. The main constraint is the legal requirement for the Initial Notice to be with the local authority at least 5 working days before work begins.

How many site visits are included?

Enough to assess compliance – your quote will state an approximate number. Typical stages include foundations, drainage, structure, fire stopping and completion. Additional visits needed because of re-inspections or programme changes may be charged as additional work.

Do I need a Principal Designer and Principal Contractor?

Yes – where more than one contractor is involved, the client must appoint a Principal Designer and Principal Contractor with responsibility for Building Regulations compliance. We ask for their details in the appointment form.

Who is responsible for compliance?

The client, designers and contractors remain responsible for compliance. We check and inspect, and can advise, but the Final Certificate is not a guarantee of compliance or a warranty of the work.

What if there’s a problem on site?

We tell you straight away, explain what needs to change and why, and re-inspect once it is fixed. Early, practical conversation avoids most issues turning into delays.

Do you work with architects and contractors as well as owners?

Absolutely. Many of our clients are architects, developers and contractors who appoint us on behalf of their client. You can fill in both the client and agent details on the appointment form.

Can you take on a higher-risk building?

No. Higher-risk buildings (18m+ or 7+ storeys with two or more homes, and certain care homes and hospitals) are regulated by the Building Safety Regulator. We will point you in the right direction.
