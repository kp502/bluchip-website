Building control

What is building control?

Building control is the independent check that building work meets the Building Regulations – so what is built is safe, healthy and accessible.

Why it matters

The Building Regulations set minimum standards for the design and construction of buildings – covering structural safety, fire safety, energy efficiency, ventilation, drainage, accessibility and more. Building control is how those standards are checked in practice: by examining the plans, inspecting the work as it progresses and, when everything is satisfactory, certifying completion.

A completion certificate is more than paperwork. Solicitors, mortgage lenders and insurers routinely ask for it when a property is sold, refinanced or insured. Work that was never signed off can delay – or derail – a sale.

What needs building control?

Most construction work in England and Wales is “building work” under the Regulations, including:

  • New buildings, extensions and loft or garage conversions
  • Structural alterations – removing walls, new openings, steelwork
  • Internal alterations to commercial buildings, including fire strategy changes
  • Changes of use, e.g. office to residential or house to HMO
  • New or altered drainage, heating, ventilation and electrical installations (subject to competent person schemes)
  • Replacement windows, roofs and thermal upgrades (again, subject to competent person schemes)

Some minor work is exempt and some trades can self-certify through a competent person scheme. If you are not sure, we are happy to advise.

Who can provide it?

Your local authority building control team, or a Registered Building Control Approver (RBCA) such as Blu Chip Compliance. It is your choice for most projects.
