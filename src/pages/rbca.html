Registered Building Control Approvers

What is an RBCA?

A Registered Building Control Approver is a private-sector building control body, registered with and regulated by the Building Safety Regulator.

The short answer

Under the Building Safety Act 2022, the private-sector building control regime in England was reformed. Since 6 April 2024, firms offering building control in England as an alternative to the local authority must be Registered Building Control Approvers – replacing the old “Approved Inspector” system (registration applications opened in October 2023). Individual surveyors working for an RBCA must themselves be Registered Building Inspectors, assessed for competence and registered with the Building Safety Regulator (part of the Health and Safety Executive).

An RBCA does the same fundamental job as local authority building control: checking that work complies with the Building Regulations. The difference is who you appoint, and how the service is delivered.

How RBCAs are regulated

  • Registered with the Building Safety Regulator (BSR) – the approver must meet competence, governance and insurance requirements.
  • Competent inspectors – building control work is carried out by individuals on the Registered Building Inspector register, at the appropriate class and category for the type of building.
  • Professional Conduct Rules – RBCAs must follow rules on impartiality, conflicts of interest and acting in the public interest.
  • Oversight – the BSR monitors performance and can investigate, impose conditions, and suspend or remove registration.
  • Insured – RBCAs must hold appropriate professional indemnity insurance.

What an RBCA cannot do

  • Higher-risk buildings. For buildings of at least 18m (or 7 storeys) containing two or more homes, and care homes and hospitals of that height, the Building Safety Regulator is the building control authority. An RBCA cannot take these on.
  • Enforcement. Only the local authority can prosecute or serve enforcement notices.
  • Regularise work that has already been carried out without building control. That is a local authority process.

Please note: if work is started before the Initial Notice has been accepted by the local authority, or if the approver cannot be satisfied that the work complies, we may not be able to issue a Final Certificate. Common questions

Is an RBCA the same as an Approved Inspector?

They are the successor. Approved Inspectors were the private-sector building control bodies under the old regime. From April 2024 the regime was replaced by Registered Building Control Approvers, subject to stricter registration and oversight by the Building Safety Regulator.

Is an RBCA Final Certificate as good as a local authority Completion Certificate?

Yes. A Final Certificate issued by a registered approver is accepted by solicitors, lenders and insurers in the same way. We always recommend keeping it safely with your property documents.

Can I switch from the local authority to an RBCA part-way through a project?

Sometimes, depending on where the work has reached and what has already been inspected. Contact us as early as possible and we will tell you honestly whether it is possible.

Does an RBCA cover Wales?

Building regulations are devolved, and the rules for private-sector building control differ between England and Wales. Please contact us about any Welsh project and we will confirm how we can help.
